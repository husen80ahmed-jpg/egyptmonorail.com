Why now

The commercial context has changed.

Egypt's monorail is no longer only an infrastructure project under construction. Commercial operations have begun, passenger-facing technology is live, and the network is entering a phase where digital access, commercial inventory and public information matter more.

Live commercial operations

Alstom announced the start of commercial service on the first portion of the East of Nile line on 6 May 2026, with 16 of 22 stations serving customers.

Alstom source ↗

Digital ticketing is operational

Indra says its automatic sales, validation and access-control technology is operational on the new monorail, with account-based ticketing designed for future digital payment and transport integration.

Indra source ↗

High-capacity urban infrastructure

Alstom states the system is designed for ultimate capacity of up to 45,000 passengers per hour, per direction — creating a substantial audience around a long-term mobility asset.

Alstom network overview ↗

A multi-layer ecosystem

The opportunity around the network is broader than trains: passenger information, payments, advertising, mobility integration, project communication and commercial partnerships all create digital entry points.
